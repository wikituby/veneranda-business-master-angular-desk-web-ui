# CRM — Clients and Relationships

The CRM side answers: who is the customer, how do we reach them, which group they belong to, and what is on their account.

**Path:** header **Client**, or Overview → **Client management**.

## Client account

A client is the account you sell to and collect from. Register at least:

- First name and second name
- Contact (phone)
- Gender, when your form requires it
- Address
- Client group, when you segment customers

Related contacts (next of kin or an alternate person) are optional and used for follow-up.

From the POS you can attach an existing client or add one without leaving the cart.

## What you keep on the account

| Record | Why it exists |
|--------|----------------|
| Profile | Identity and how to reach the client |
| Group | Shared rules, such as who may buy on credit |
| Sales history | Every order and document raised for them |
| Balance | What is still unpaid after receipts |
| Transactions | Payments, including reversed ones |

Search the registry by name. On the POS client picker, an empty search lists clients so you are not stuck on one name.

## Client groups

Groups are how the CRM organises customers: retail, wholesale, staff, or a house account. Some groups need a higher privilege before a user may assign a client to them. If assignment is refused, ask an administrator.

Use groups for reporting and for credit policy. Do not create a new client for every visit. Reuse the account so history stays in one place.

## Orders and the relationship

An **order** is the commercial file for one checkout. Documents on that order (quotation through delivery) belong to that relationship.

- A **new sale** creates a **new order**. Staff do not attach today’s document to an old order from a list.
- An **edit** keeps the order and may move it to a different client.
- Turning an order **off** freezes edits and new documents. History, view, print, and share remain.

## Follow-up

Use the client profile and the products orders list together:

1. Find the client.
2. Open their orders for the date range you care about (today, last month, last 7 days, or a custom from–to).
3. Open a document to view, print, share, or edit.
4. If money is still due, receive a payment on that order or record it from finance. Reversing a payment keeps the row and recalculates paid and debt.

## Credit

Selling for less than the total is allowed when a client is attached. The difference stays as amount due on the order. Clear debt before extending more credit when your group rules say so. A credit-allowed group can be an exception. That exception is an administrator setting, not a cashier override.

## Good CRM practice

- One real client, one account. Avoid duplicate names with different phones.
- Attach the client before you save a document.
- Do not type the client only into notes. Notes do not build the account.
- When a contact changes, update the profile. Old documents keep the name they were saved with until you edit them.
